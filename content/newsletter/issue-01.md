## This Month In Tennis

**We found the pro's racquet board at the Lincoln Challenger**

At the Lincoln Challenger in July, the stringing desk posted every player's setup. Racquet, string, tension, all of it on one board.

![The racquet board at the Lincoln Challenger listing every player's racquet, string and tension](issue-01-board.jpg)
*The racquet board at the Lincoln Challenger*

**Not a single Pure Drive or Clash on the board** - the two racquets marketed hardest to everyday players. The pros were overwhelmingly on control frames: Blades, VCOREs, EZONE 98s, Pro Staffs. And the tensions? Almost every setup was in the low 50s, averaging about 51 lbs.

>m WHAT IT MEANS FOR YOU: the players who hit the biggest string the loosest, and play frames built for precision, not power.

## From The Court

**Your elbow feels tension you can't**

Every extra pound of string tension stiffens the string bed, and a stiffer bed sends more shock into your forearm on every off-center hit. For most of us that's about a third of our shots. Which is why "my arm's a little sore after tennis" is usually a stringing problem before it's a technique problem.

**Try this in your next session, before you change anything.**

Play normally, and pay attention to how your forearm feels the next morning. Not during play, when adrenaline hides everything. The next morning. If the answer is "a little achy," ask your stringer for 2 to 3 lbs less at your next restring, and give it two sessions before you judge it.

**Tension Range**

This is the range most players in each situation land in from our research:

![Tension ranges by situation, from 48 to 56 lbs depending on level, swing speed and string type](issue-01-tension-chart.png)

Two things here surprise people. Stronger players string **higher**, not lower, because they generate their own pace and need the control. And if your arm hurts, the answer is usually a softer string at a lower tension, not a stiffer setup to keep the ball in.

Your own numbers are in your report, and they account for more than this chart can.
